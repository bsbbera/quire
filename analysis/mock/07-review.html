Eleven weeks is a long time for a light to keep itself, and the log said nothing about it. Cormack read the last page twice on the boat over, holding it out of the spray, and found only the ordinary entries a keeper makes when there is nothing to make an entry about.

The tender put him off at the landing and did not wait. He watched it turn in the sound with the particular haste of a crew that has somewhere else to be, and then he carried his box up the hundred and forty steps cut into the rock, and let himself in through a door that was not locked.

Inside, everything was in its place. That was the first thing, and it took him some time to understand why it troubled him. The kitchen was swept. He set the box down and favoured his right leg going up the last flight, and stood a while in the lamp room with his hand on the rail.

He counted the oil again in the morning, and again on the second evening, and the figure did not move. Somebody had been burning it at exactly the rate the book required, and writing none of it down, for seventy-seven days.
